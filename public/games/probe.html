Phone Probe

What this phone can actually do, measured on the phone rather than looked up. Nine checks. Nothing here changes any game; the page only asks the browser questions and reports the answers.

How to run it: turn the phone to landscape first, then work down the page and press every button. Tests 5 and 6 ask the system for permission, so they need a real tap. The last panel collects everything into one block to send back.

01 Screen and viewport

The number that decides everything is the width of one character. The screen is 64 characters across, so a character gets the canvas width divided by 64. Below about 6px a 5x7 glyph stops being readable, and the menus, the high score table and the name entry are all text.

02 Is it readable

Drawn with the games' own 5x7 character generator, through the games' own paint routine, then scaled down exactly as the page scales it. This is not a mock-up of the text: it is the text. Judge it with your eyes, that is the measurement. Three sizes, because rotating the phone only helps if the height is not already spent on a title, a button bar and the address bar.

03 Can it keep up

This runs the real Z80 from the game pages, on a program built from the same things the games do: a 256-byte LDIR block move like the buffer-to-screen blit, 8-bit arithmetic through memory, LD A,R for the random source, and a counted loop. A Model I ran 1,774,000 T-states a second, so anything above 1x is fast enough.

04 Touch, and controls over the picture

A mock Sea Dragon screen with the proposed overlay on top: thumbstick at the left, two buttons at the right, both semi-transparent and both sitting on the picture rather than below it. Drive it, then do one thing deliberately: hold the stick and press both buttons at once, so three fingers are down together. The tanks need three, two tread levers and fire, and the count below is the only way to know the phone will report them.

05 Fullscreen

Worth real height: losing the address bar is the difference between a readable landscape canvas and a cramped one. Press this, look at the size it reports, then come out again.

06 Locking to landscape

Asking the phone to stay in landscape. On some browsers this only works while already fullscreen, which is why it comes after test 5: if it refuses, the name of the refusal tells us whether it is unsupported or just out of order.

07 Tilt

For Tank Zone, tilting forward, back, left and right instead of working two tread levers with two thumbs. Press the button, then hold the phone the way you would play and tilt it for a few seconds. The dashed circle is a 12° deadzone: inside it nothing fires. The four boxes light the way the treads would drive.

Three separate APIs can carry tilt and phones disagree about which one works, so all three are listened to at once and reported separately. A phone that fires events full of nothing is a different problem from one that fires none, and both are different from a working sensor.

If they all come back empty, the lines under why say whether the sensor is switched off or simply absent. Switched off is usually the browser rather than the phone: privacy browsers such as Brave block motion sensors by default as a fingerprinting defence, and report the permission as denied while the hardware sits there working. Look under Site settings → Motion sensors, and at the padlock beside the address for this site.
