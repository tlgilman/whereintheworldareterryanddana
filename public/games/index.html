TRS-80 RETRO GAMES

Lifted off my own floppies and put back on their feet, in your browser.

ARCADE

Nine games Wayne Westmoreland and I wrote for the TRS-80 between 1981 and 1983, in the order we wrote them. Not ports: the original machine code is inside each page and a Z80 runs it.

TANK ZONE 2000
1981
Battlezone on a TRS-80, under its first name. The pyramids are still pyramids.
A and ; forward · Z and . backward · A and . turn right · Z and ; turn left · Space fire
ARMORED PATROL
1981
The same tank after our publisher asked us for houses instead of pyramids and a robot instead of a saucer.
A and ; forward · Z and . backward · A and . turn right · Z and ; turn left · Space fire
DEFENDER
1981
Williams’ Defender on a TRS-80, before we renamed it.
Arrow keys move · Space fire · Enter smart bomb · Home or Left Ctrl hyperspace
THE ELIMINATOR
1981
The same game after Williams took a percentage, at which point it became the official TRS-80 Defender.
Arrow keys move · Space fire · Enter smart bomb · Home or Left Ctrl hyperspace
SEA DRAGON
1982
This is our own idea, and our favorite. A submarine, 1,858 columns of cavern, and one lungful of air.
Arrow keys move · Space torpedo · Enter missile
REAR GUARD
1982
Up and down only. Commissioned by Adventure International, and the one we liked least.
Up and Down move · Space fire · Enter shields · Home or Left Ctrl smart bomb
DONKEY KONG
1982
We wrote this one for the fun of it. Nintendo never gave permission, so it was never sold.
Arrow keys move · Space jump
ZAXXON
1983
Licensed from Sega, with a Tandy distribution deal that manufacturing problems on our end cut short.
Arrow keys move · Space fire
ROBOT: KING OF THE HILL
1983
Our Donkey Kong reskinned, released as Smith & Jones Software. The assembler left the symbol APEPLAC in the code.
Arrow keys move · Space jump

ADVENTURES

Every one is a single self-contained file. Two-word commands. Type MAP to see where you have been; the game autosaves every turn.

The arcade games are mine and Wayne Westmoreland's, written for the TRS-80 between 1981 and 1983 and recovered from my own disks and one cassette. Wayne has since released them into the public domain. Robot: King of the Hill went out under the name Smith & Jones Software; it is our Donkey Kong reskinned, after Nintendo declined permission.
Thirteen of the adventures are the original Adams series, read straight off the disk images - the game data is byte-for-byte what the TRS-80 loaded.
The last three are third-party games written with the same engine: a James Bond adventure, and two by Bruce Hansen built with THE ADVENTURE SYSTEM.
Eight of them are not treasure hunts and finish on an event rather than a score. In those SCORE is not a command, or the game answers it in its own words.
